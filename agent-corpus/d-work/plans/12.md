### Mutation (the same craft-zone PR #96, one new commit; VERSION stays 0.3.1, still unreleased)
The Positioning section becomes:

> ## Positioning
> Prompt Therapy is not a tool, and it does not compete with tools. It is a service that gives people insight into
> what they observed an AI agent do, drawn from what other people observed.
>
> Its competition is a general-purpose agent used out of the box, Claude Code and others like it. Asked why something
> went wrong, such an agent can already infer a likely root cause, and it gets better at it with every release. What it
> cannot show is where that answer comes from: whether anything behind it came from people who struggled the same way,
> how many of them there were, and whether they would put it the same way. The user gets a conclusion with no evidence
> they can inspect.
>
> Prompt Therapy's difference is the provenance of its evidence. Every pattern it offers is tagged with its sources:
> first-hand observations from real users, each kept only with that user's consent, in words that user confirmed, and
> counted, so a user can see how many people reported it and when. The difference holds for as long as a general agent
> cannot show the same.
>
> For v1.0 this means:
> - each canon entry the walk offers shows its sources: how many people reported it, their observation ids and when,
>   never who (see Never). An entry with one source says so;
> - a cause is offered as a hypothesis together with that evidence, never on the agent's say-so. Advice with no source
>   behind it is not the product;
> - "certified" means only what the record shows: first-hand, consented, confirmed in the reporter's own words and
>   de-identified. No outside body certifies anything, and the service never claims more;
> - the walk still ends with something to carry back to one's own agent: a cause the user recognises, and a prompt or
>   practice to try;
> - if general agents start showing consented first-hand evidence for a pattern, that entry's difference falls. The
>   operate and evolve play-books watch for this (`dmc:feedback-route`, `dmc:service-retire`).

The open questions gain:
> 4. Whether people value seeing where the evidence comes from. The positioning rests on it, and it is unproven: the
>    user-research play-book tests it before v1.0 ships.
> 5. Whether a reporter's confirmed words may be shown to other users, or only counted. v1.0 shows counts, ids and dates
>    alone, because consent today covers keeping the words, not showing them.
