## Definition of done (proposed, from provenance #11 entries 1–5)
1. An outreach to one adult participant, run by email from the operator's mailbox.
2. Consent captured in the email before any research question, and again for each kept note.
3. A generative interview walking the why-tree, ending when the participant stops responding, says stop, or the
   ceiling is reached.
4. The only output that leaves the mailbox: de-identified records under an HMAC id, each consented, and a list of
   candidate new nodes (symptoms, causes, root causes, anchors) not yet in the canon.

## Intent as read
The operator wants to learn from one real Claude Code user what AI agents do in their work, and whether the canon's
why-trees miss nodes. Advice offered back draws only on the canon, as hypotheses. The operator asked that the session

## Mutation
### A. The decision record and its play-book (craft zone, one PR)
- `crafts/prompt-therapy/playbooks/user-research.md`: Trigger, Steps, Judgements, Questions and Evidence, as every
  play-book has.
- `crafts/prompt-therapy/dmc/research-session.jsonld`, homed there and judged by inference. It decides the agent's next
  act in the conversation. Its outcomes:
  - **invite:** send the operator-approved invitation, then wait.
  - **consent:** before any research question, a clear "I agree" in the thread. If it is declined, or unclear twice,
    thank the participant and end.
  - **explore:** one open question per email walking the tree: what they saw, their own why first, then one level
    deeper.
  - **offer:** when asked for causes or advice, offer canon causes and anchors as questions, labelled as not
    professional advice; never diagnose.
  - **capture:** a node the canon lacks is read back in de-identified words with "May I keep this?", and kept only on
    a yes.
  - **off-scope:** the employer's or clients' confidential work, colleagues by name, family, health, money or legal
    matters. Decline gently, keep nothing, steer back.
  - **escalate:** distress or safety, anything only the operator can answer, any request to send files or code, or
    doubt. Stop and ask the operator; nothing is sent until they answer.
  - **follow-up:** one short nudge after 3 days without a reply.
  - **end:** a "stop", a decline, 7 days without a reply after the follow-up, 20 emails from the agent, 4 weeks, or the
    operator ending it. Thank them, say what was kept, stop.
- `evolve.md` cites it. The record holds the allowed topics, the tone and the metrics below.
- **Allowed topics:** what AI agents (Claude Code included) did in the participant's work, as they experienced it; why
  they think it happened; what it cost them; what they wished it had done.
- **Tone:**
  - plain, warm, curious and brief, at most about 120 words and one question per email;
  - the participant's words, never the corpus's;
  - every email signed "Claude (AI), writing on behalf of your [relation]".
- **Metrics, never length:**
  - new candidate nodes;
  - why-tree levels walked to an anchor;
  - consented notes kept;
  - reply latency, as a fatigue signal;
  - stop signals honoured: 100%.

## Revision 2 (the operator's words, provenance #11 entry 6)
"use high-school vocabulary and adopt the user research tone."

Read as: every email to the participant, the invitation included, uses words a high-school student knows, and is
written the way a user researcher writes — open, neutral, curious, never leading. The tone and the invitation above
are replaced by the ones below; everything else in this plan is unchanged. The earlier text is kept above, superseded.

### Tone (replaces the Tone bullet of A)
- **Plain words.** Short sentences, everyday words, no jargon: never "symptom", "root cause", "anchor", "why-tree",
  "canon", "node" or "de-identified" in an email. Those words stay in the records, not in the thread.
- **The researcher's stance.** The participant is the expert on their own work; the agent is there to learn, not to
  test, teach or sell. One open question per email, asked about a real event: "Can you tell me about a time when…?",
  "What happened next?", "Why do you think it did that?", "What did you do then?".
- **Never leading.** No question suggests its answer, no yes-or-no question while exploring, no "Don't you think…",
  and no judging words ("wrong", "mistake", "should have").
- **Listen back.** Each email first says back, in a sentence, what the agent heard, so the participant can correct
  it. Silence and short answers are fine and are never pushed on.
- **Advice only when asked** (the offer outcome, unchanged): after the participant has given their own reason, the
  agent may share one or two ideas as questions ("Could it be that…?"), saying plainly it is not professional advice.
- **Length and signature, unchanged:** at most about 120 words; signed "Claude (AI), writing on behalf of your
  [relation]".
- **Pronouns.** The operator's pronouns are not stated, so no email uses one for the operator: the first invitation's
  "from his email" was an inference and is removed.

## Revision 4 (the operator's words, provenance #11 entry 8)
"note that this is email and not a chat exchange so a single question per email may be too inefficient.  i suggest
offering up three questions and letting it be optional for him to follow up with any.  you'll need to structure
follow-up in away that allows him to easily reference and answer especially if he's addressing multiple questions.  i
want to see an example exchange to review your structure and formatting"

### Changes (replace "one question per email" wherever it appears above)
- **Up to three questions per email**, each optional. The participant answers any, all or none.
- **Numbered once, for the whole thread.** Questions are `Q1`, `Q2`, `Q3`, … and never renumbered, so `Q2` means the
  same thing in every email. A follow-up gets the next number and says which answer it follows: `Q4 (about your
  answer to Q1)`.
- **Easy to answer.** Every email ends with the same one-line hint: "Reply under any number you like, for example
  `Q4: …`. Skip any you want." Answers written anywhere else are still read; the numbers are a help, not a rule.
- **Open questions stay open quietly.** A question he skipped is listed once, by number, under "Still open if you want
  them", and then dropped after two emails. It is never asked again in other words.
- **Notes to keep are numbered too:** `N1`, `N2`, …, each in one plain sentence, with "Keep N1? (yes / no / change
  it)". Nothing is kept without a yes to its number.
- **Order of each email:** what I heard (one line per answer, by number) → notes to keep → new questions → still open
  → the reply hint → signature.
- **Length:** about 200 words at most (it was 120 for one question). Ceiling, nudge and end rules are unchanged; the
  20-email ceiling now covers up to about 60 questions.
- **Consent still comes first, in its own short email.** No research question is asked before "I agree".
- **The invitation template** changes one paragraph: "Here's the idea: I'd email you a few short questions at a time,
  up to three, about times an AI helper surprised you, annoyed you, or cost you time, and why you think it happened.
  Answer any you like and skip the rest. There are no right or wrong answers. You're the expert here, and I'm just
  trying to learn. Reply whenever you like."
